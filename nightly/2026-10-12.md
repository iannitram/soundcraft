## 1. Summary for Ian

- **9 bug fixes are on branches, each with a test that fails before the fix.** The worst two for audiobooks:
  - Importing a file longer than about 93 minutes at a different sample rate (e.g. a 2-hour 44.1 kHz master into a 48 kHz session) **silently dropped everything after minute 93**.
  - Saving a session **clipped any audio above 0 dBFS**, even in 32-bit float files.
- Editing fixes:
  - Splitting a clip lost its volume envelope.
  - Copy carried stray fades.
  - Crossfades dipped to silence when one clip had no spare audio.
  - Paste, Duplicate and Repeat broke crossfades.
  - AudioSuite processing dropped the clip's gain.
- **Playback on a device running at a different rate than the session** (for example a Mac at 44.1 kHz with a 48 kHz session) **buzzed**: one sample per audio block was pulled towards zero. Fixed.
- **Loudness meter checked against an independent reference implementation:** within 0.02 LU at 44.1–192 kHz, so it is trustworthy for Match Loudness.
- **WAV, AIFF and FLAC round-trips on all three test files are bit-exact.**
- **Speed:** a benchmark tool now exists. Making import resampling use all cores cut a 60-minute resample from 115 s to 31 s on this 4-core machine; the Z13's 16 cores should do better. The "max optimization" build setting made no measurable difference.
- **MP3 export:** no usable permissively licensed MP3 encoder exists in Rust yet (I tested the best candidate: too slow and low quality). **Your call** on writing our own (see Needs Ian).
- **Pull-request CI** (G-14) workflow is written, checked locally and pushed.
- Design write-ups are in STATE.md: disk streaming (G-06), GPU spectrogram, Linux audio, and the Waveform Editor (crate audit, dependency strategy, noise reduction, Match Loudness, DeepFilterNet speech enhancement).
