## 1. Summary for Ian

- **The bug review of item 1 is finished:** every file in its code areas has now been read once. Tonight covered the five areas run 1 only skimmed.
- **Most important new find: long bounces were silently cut at about 93 minutes.**
  - Bounce Mix allows 4 hours, but the renderer stops at a memory cap equal to about 93 minutes of 48 kHz stereo. A 2-hour audiobook master would have come out 93 minutes long, with no error.
  - The same cap made Gain/Effects Render, Consolidate, Commit and Export Clips lose the end of long clips.
  - Strip Silence was worse: it treated the cut-off part as silence and deleted those clips.

  - In Shuffle mode, Clear and Paste left volume automation behind, so rides drifted off their words. Upstream has filed this as issue #232.
  - Trim to File Boundaries (and Trim to Fill Selection) grew a clip over its neighbour, so you heard two layers. Upstream issues #229 and #228.
  - Moving crossfaded clips together broke the crossfade.
  - Two armed tracks with similar names (e.g. "Host?" and "Host!") recorded into the same file, so one take was lost.
  - Bounce Stems overwrote stems of tracks whose names clash.
  - Broadcast-WAV timestamps were wrong after importing at a different sample rate.
- **Web version:** saving a session longer than about 46 minutes "worked" but the file could never be reopened. Saving now refuses with a clear message.
- **Speed:** the loudness measurement that runs after every bounce, and that Match Loudness will need, is **4× faster** with identical results.
- **MP3 export still waits on your decision.** One new fact for it: minimp3, a small MP3 decoder, is public domain (CC0). Its tables could seed a clean-room encoder without licence problems (details in Needs Ian).

- **`perf/fast-loudness`** (on top of `perf/bench-harness`)
  - `LoudnessMeter::process` now:
    - works channel by channel up to each 100 ms block;
    - runs the true-peak filter vectorised across samples;
    - for long offline calls, measures each channel on its own thread (scoped threads, not the realtime mixer's pool; no threads on wasm).
  - Output is bit-identical to the old frame-at-a-time code. The test compares every reading and the internal state on mono, stereo and 5.1, with NaN/inf input and uneven block sizes.
  - Also adds a `loudness_whole` bench stage.
  - PR-worthy: yes; lift only the dsp commit.

Numbers (20 min of 48 kHz stereo, release build; cloud CPU, not the Z13; relative only):

| | Before | After | Speed-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole-file call (what a bounce does) | 5.15 s | 1.26 s | 4.1× |
| 4096-frame blocks | 4.81 s | 2.64 s | 1.8× |

Of the old block time, about 1.1 s was the K-weighting filters (one serial chain per channel, can't be split further) and about 2.4 s the true-peak interpolation, which is now about 1.5 s. AVX2/AVX-512 runtime dispatch would need `unsafe` (the crate forbids it), so this stays portable SIMD that the compiler picks.
